# Town of Glover Website — Phase 1 Content Audit Workbook

Prepared for Jenny and Olivia  
Working draft based on the public-site capture completed July 30, 2026

This is the editable working document for the minimum viable website. It reorganizes the captured source material into proposed pages and preserves the actual operational information that must be reviewed before design and build. The complete crawl, original documents, images, and raw page captures remain unchanged in this archive.

## How to use this workbook

For every page, review the draft copy and mark each item as Keep, Rewrite, Remove, Verify, or Phase 2. Bracketed notes such as `[VERIFY]` are editorial flags, not website copy. Names, contact information, hours, fees, schedules, legal guidance, external links, and document status should be confirmed by the Town before launch.

Suggested review owners:

- Jenny: content structure, plain-language editing, page hierarchy, calls to action, and build requirements.
- Olivia: municipal accuracy, missing information, content ownership, and Town approval.
- Town staff or board owner: final verification of operational, legal, financial, and emergency information.

### Glover Recycling Center

Recycling is free in Glover. Materials must be sorted into the categories listed below and must be reasonably clean. Sort materials before arriving to save time and space at the Recycling Center.

Location: `[CONTENT NEEDED — no street address was captured on the current page]`  
Coordinator: Theresa Perron — 802-525-7199

### Hours

Winter, December 1–March 31:

- Saturday, 9:00 a.m.–noon

Summer, April 1–November 30:

- Wednesday, noon–3:00 p.m.
- Saturday, 9:00 a.m.–noon

`[VERIFY: holiday, weather, and closure procedures.]`

### Prepare materials before arriving

- Sort materials at home.
- Rinse containers so they are reasonably clean; soap washing is not required.
- Flatten corrugated-cardboard boxes.
- Remove prohibited materials and plastic bags.

### Paper

Newspapers, magazines, office paper, junk mail, catalogs, telephone books, glossy inserts, paperback books, colored and white paper, wrapping paper, and paper envelopes may be mixed together.

Do not include brown kraft paper bags or boxboard in this category. Do not include paper with metal foil.

### Corrugated cardboard, kraft bags, and boxboard

This category includes corrugated cardboard, brown kraft paper bags, and non-corrugated boxboard such as cereal boxes. Flatten boxes. Remove strings, plastic handles, and bags with plastic layers. Excess tape should be removed; staples and some tape are acceptable.

Do not include wax-coated cardboard or cardboard soiled with food, including pizza boxes.

### Metal cans and foil

- Tin or steel cans: labels are acceptable; rinse clean. Flattening is not required. Keep separate from aluminum. A magnet sticks to tin or steel, but not aluminum.
- Aluminum cans, foil, and food trays: labels are acceptable; rinse clean.

### Glass

Glass bottles and jars of all colors are accepted. Rinse them and remove lids.

Do not include crystal, plastic/metal/wood lids, or light bulbs. Fluorescent bulbs are handled as a separate category.

### Plastic containers

The current page states that #1, #2, #3, #4, and some #5 plastic containers up to two gallons are accepted. #5 is limited to food and drink containers. Labels may remain; containers should be rinsed.

Do not include automotive-product containers, vinyl siding, or Styrofoam in this category.

# Page 13 — Meetings and Public Records

**Proposed URL:** `/meetings-records/`  
**Primary purpose:** Provide a searchable, authoritative index of agendas, draft and approved minutes, recordings, packets, notices, and related public documents.  
**Current sources:** year pages, committee pages, individual posts, the Town Documents page, and the Select Board Google Drive recording folder.

## Draft page copy

### Find a meeting or public record

Search or filter records by board or committee, meeting date, year, record type, and status. Original documents remain the authoritative public record.

Suggested filters:

- Board or committee
- Meeting date or date range
- Record type: agenda, draft minutes, approved minutes, corrected minutes, recording, packet, notice, or related document
- Meeting type: regular, special, emergency, hearing, site visit, work session, or Town Meeting
- Calendar year

Each result should show:

- Clear title
- Meeting date and time
- Board or committee
- Record type and approval status
- Publication or update date
- Direct link to the original file
- Recording link, when available
- Related or superseding record, when applicable

### Record status language

- **Agenda:** the published plan for a meeting; subject to change as allowed by law.
- **Draft minutes:** not yet approved and may be corrected.
- **Approved minutes:** approved by the responsible public body.
- **Corrected or superseded:** replaced by a later official version; retain the earlier file in the archive but direct readers to the controlling version.

`[VERIFY these descriptions and public-record language with the Town.]`

### Local Minutes dependency

Local Minutes publicly describes itself as a service that gathers municipal public documents, makes them searchable through an AI-assisted interface, and cites source documents. It also offers a separate Draft Minutes tool that helps boards and clerks turn meeting recordings into draft minutes.

Public information reviewed in August 2026 does not expose a documented integration API, feed specification, or webhook that can safely be assumed for the Town website. Local Minutes says it automatically checks municipal websites for new public documents, in some cases nightly. Its terms mention that accounts may be provided API keys, but that statement is not technical documentation or confirmation that a municipal publishing API is available.

For Phase 1, the Town website should therefore remain able to publish and index official records independently. Local Minutes can be connected when the vendor confirms a supported integration method.

### Questions for the Local Minutes partner

- Does Local Minutes provide a supported public or partner API?
- Can it send a webhook when a document is created, approved, or uploaded?
- Can it provide a JSON or RSS/Atom feed of Glover records?
- What is the stable unique ID for a meeting and for each document version?
- Does the response include board, meeting date, record type, draft/approved status, title, source-file URL, recording URL, and last-updated date?
- Can the source file remain in Google Drive, or must it also be published on the Town website?
- Can the Town website link directly to the original file without requiring a Local Minutes login?
- How are revised, approved, corrected, or deleted records communicated?
- Is there an embeddable search interface, and can its accessibility and visual styling be controlled?
- What are authentication, rate-limit, privacy, retention, uptime, export, and vendor-exit terms?
- Can the Town export all metadata and source links if the service changes or ends?
- Which system is considered the official record, and which is only a discovery or drafting tool?

### Recommended fallback for Phase 1

Create a native meeting-record type in the new website with the fields listed above. Staff can paste a Google Drive or Local Minutes source URL into each record until an automated connection is confirmed. This delivers a searchable public archive without making launch dependent on an undocumented integration.

### Accuracy notice

Local Minutes states that its AI-generated summaries may contain errors and that original public documents remain the controlling source. If Local Minutes search or summaries are presented on the Town website, the interface should preserve that distinction and keep direct source-document links prominent.
